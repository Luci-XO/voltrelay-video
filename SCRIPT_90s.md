# VoltRelay: 90-second pitch script (~225 words, speak at a normal pace)

**[0:00 – 0:12] Hook**
VoltRelay grew fast. Battery swaps went up almost three times and revenue tripled. But failed swaps nearly doubled, more new riders left, and margins were under pressure. Leadership had six suspects and didn't know which one to fix.

**[0:12 – 0:22] What I did**
I cleaned 3.8 million swap events, including test stations, duplicates, impossible sensor readings and mis-tagged tickets. Then I compared affected and unaffected groups for each suspect.

**[0:22 – 0:42] Root cause one: old chargers in the heat**
Failures are a summer problem. Gen1 chargers take 88 minutes to charge a pack, but 190 minutes at 50 degrees, so cabinets run out of charged batteries. Gen1 stations fail 12.7 percent of swaps, against 5.1 percent at new Gen3 stations, and most of them are in Jaipur, Delhi and Hyderabad, the hottest cities.

**[0:42 – 0:55] Root cause two: a bad battery batch**
Three lots from the new supplier fell to 61 percent health. They give 19 percent less range, and 4.79 crore rupees of packs were retired in one day.

**[0:55 – 1:05] Root cause three: a discount that bought nothing**
The largest fleet partner's discount went from 12 to 28 percent. Profit per swap fell 25 percent, with no extra volume.

**[1:05 – 1:15] Churn**
New riders with under 5 percent failed swaps in month one churn at 10.7 percent. Above 20 percent, nearly 40 percent leave.

**[1:15 – 1:30] Recommendation**
So the budget should go to replacing Gen1 chargers in hot cities before next summer, recovering costs from the supplier, and tying fleet discounts to off-peak use. The expansion itself was never the problem. Thank you.
